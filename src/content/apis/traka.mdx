## Problem and constraints

Amaka runs a provision store in a Lagos market. On a typical day she sells bread, sachet water, and rice — some for cash, some on credit to regulars who repay weekly. She has no reliable way to know if she made a profit that day. She discovers she's out of stock only when a customer asks for an item she no longer has. Several customers owe her money, but she has no consistent record of who or how much.

When Amaka needs ₦50,000 to restock ahead of a busy season, no financial institution will lend to her — she has no record proving her business is viable. This pattern is widespread: **Nigeria's 39.6 million micro and small enterprises generate 48% of national GDP, yet eight in ten fail before their fifth year**, most managed entirely through memory or paper. The core issue is not a lack of ambition, but a lack of financial visibility.


## Key decisions

- **AI-Powered Inventory Creation**: Uploading product catalogs manually is a massive barrier for informal merchants. Instead, merchants snap photos of their shelves, and Gemini 3.5 Flash automatically extracts structured product names and variants. No typing, no forms to fill.
- **Virtual accounts as payment identity**: Every merchant gets a dedicated 10-digit virtual account at signup. Incoming transfers auto-reconcile via webhook — zero manual entry for digital payments.
- **Dual reconciliation paths for transfers**:
  - **Sale reconciliation** → product basket (product_id + qty) → computes revenue/profit from selling vs cost price
  - **Debt reconciliation** → debtor_id + repayment_amount → reduces outstanding balance
  Both produce a fully reconciled `Transaction` with audit trail.
- **In-memory Background Scheduling for Weekly Summaries**: Instead of spinning up heavy external workers like Celery, I utilized APScheduler within the FastAPI lifespan to generate and dispatch weekly business summaries. This keeps infrastructure simple while ensuring cron jobs run reliably.

## The hard part

**1. AI Product Identification from Images**
Uploading a product catalogue one-by-one is tedious for market vendors. 
**Solution**: Used Gemini 3.5 Flash to automatically extract structured product names and variants (e.g., "Peak Full Cream Milk Tin (400g)") directly from merchant photos. Built a custom prompt enforcing standard commercial naming conventions and catching blurry images ("UNREADABLE") to ensure inventory data remains clean.

**2. Transaction reconciliation state machine under concurrency**
Transfer webhook → `UnallocatedTransaction` (pending). Merchant confirms "Sale" or "Debt" → validate basket/debtor → create `Transaction` (reconciled) → update inventory/debtor atomically.
**Challenge**: Double-tap or concurrent confirmations on same reference could lead to double-crediting a debtor or double-decrementing stock.
**Solution**: Rather than heavy database locking, the service queries specifically for `status == "unallocated"`. The first request updates the status to `"reconciled"` and commits. Any concurrent request instantly hits a 404 "Unallocated transaction not found", making the reconciliation completely idempotent.


## Technical lessons

**1. Domain-Driven Architecture for Scalability**
Instead of the traditional layered approach—where all database models are dumped in one folder and all routes in another—I structured the codebase into distinct domain modules (e.g., `accounts`, `inventory`, `transactions`, `debtors`). Every feature is completely self-contained with its own router, service logic, and data model. This prevents the codebase from turning into a tangled mess as it grows, making it incredibly easy for new engineers to jump in and own a specific vertical slice of the application.

**2. Dual Reconciliation Paths to Prevent Schema Bloat**
In Traka, money moving isn't just a generic event; it represents either a product being sold or a debtor paying back what they owe. Instead of creating one massive `Transaction` database table filled with optional, nullable fields for every possible scenario, I built explicit, separate service paths: `reconcile_as_sale` and `reconcile_as_debt`. This enforces strict business rules at the code level (a sale must have a product basket, a repayment must have a debtor ID), entirely eliminating the risk of saving invalid transaction states to the database.

## Results and Impact

**Engineering Outcomes:**
- **Rapid Inventory Onboarding:** By integrating Gemini 3.5 Flash for image extraction, merchants can create their product catalogs just by snapping photos, completely removing the friction of manual data entry.
- **Robust State Management:** The decision to use strict dual-path reconciliation (`Sale` vs `Debt`) and `unallocated` state tracking proved highly effective at maintaining database integrity without relying on complex, heavy locking mechanisms.
- **Guaranteed Tenant Isolation:** The strict dependency injection of `get_current_account_id` into every service layer ensures zero cross-tenant data leaks across the entire platform.

**Strategic Outcomes:**
- **Complete financial picture:** Unlike traditional tools (which rely on manual entry and skew heavily towards digital payments), Traka captures cash sales and debts with the same friction as bank transfers. This creates a much fuller, more accurate transaction volume.
- **Lender-ready data:** By providing strict audit trails for both sales and debt repayments, Traka generates a verified financial history—creating proof of viability for ₦50K+ restock loans.
- **Platform trajectory:** Bookkeeping → Payments → Credit → Financial infrastructure for Nigeria's 39.6M micro-enterprises.
